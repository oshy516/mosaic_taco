\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Existing approaches to optimizing loop nests and tensor programs can be broadly divided into \emph{schedule optimization} and \emph{execution-space optimization}. Schedule optimization has been studied extensively, through polyhedral scheduling~\cite{ppcg,polly,tc,polygeist,akg}, specialized tiling transformations for stencil computations~\cite{splitover,hybridtile,diamond}, and schedule-template search~\cite{halide,tvm}. Execution-space optimization remains dominated by heuristics and empirical tuning. \name is a member of the second class only: it consumes a transformation schedule already fixed by one of these upstream systems and never searches or revises it, so it is complementary to, rather than competing with, schedule optimization.

\par\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}\textit{Analytical optimization.} Analytical tile size selection has a long history on CPUs~\cite{mckinley-tss,lam-tss,pingali-tss,tss-revisited}, later formulated using polynomial~\cite{pluto-tss} or geometric programming~\cite{rajopadhye-gp,rajopadhye-igp} within frameworks such as Pluto~\cite{pluto}, PPCG~\cite{ppcg}, and PolyMage~\cite{polymage}, or decoupled from compiler internals through an MILP formulation by TileMind~\cite{tilemind}. These methods target CPU cache residency and focus on tile sizes alone.

GPU execution-space optimization is more challenging because tile sizes interact with thread-block configurations, shared-memory allocation, occupancy, and register pressure. A recent work~\cite{ppcg-tss} incorporates tile-size selection into PPCG's scheduling formulation. EATSS~\cite{eatss} derives a non-linear integer formulation per affine program and solves it with Z3~\cite{z3}, choosing tile sizes that satisfy the architectural resource limits under a joint latency--energy objective. It splits array references between the L1 cache and shared memory by whether they admit coalesced access, and takes the thread-block size to be the product of the tile sizes of the outer parallel dimensions. Among published analytical GPU tile-size selectors with available implementations, it is the closest baseline in scope to \name. The differences follow from the two choices above. \name treats the thread-block dimensions as decision variables separate from the tile sizes, so the register-blocking factor \(\rblk_d=\tile_d/b_d\) is selected rather than implied. It enumerates staging scenarios instead of assigning memory spaces by a fixed rule, and quantizes its objective by the integer wave count instead of optimizing a smooth expression of the tile sizes. TileMind targets CPU cache residency rather than GPU execution-space selection. \name applies a compiler-agnostic analytical core to compiler-provided schedule metadata through compiler-specific adapters.

\par\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}\textit{Kernel-specific models.} Several analytical models target specific kernels such as GEMM and convolution~\cite{anatomy,li-gemm,blis,saday-conv,conv-cache}, achieving high accuracy but generalizing poorly. \name instead targets a broader class of affine kernels, including contractions, reductions, and stencil computations.

\par\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}\textit{Search-based optimization.} Modern systems increasingly rely on empirical search: early kernel-specific autotuners such as PHiPAC and ATLAS explore parameters for dense linear algebra~\cite{phipac,atlas}, OpenTuner composes general search techniques~\cite{opentuner}, and more recent systems such as Ansor~\cite{ansor}, MetaSchedule~\cite{metaschedule}, and related tuners for Halide~\cite{automatichalide} and Triton~\cite{triton} generate and evaluate many candidate configurations. Learning-based and dynamic-programming approaches further approximate this space with predictive models or recursive search~\cite{hctuner,yuki-tss,Jangda2020dp,shirako-tss}; all can be viewed as alternative ways of exploring the same execution-space optimization problem.

Rather than searching over configurations, \name directly models GPU resource interactions and solves for execution parameters analytically; \autoref{sec:eval} compares it against the PPCG heuristic, the energy-aware EATSS analytical selector, and the search-based MetaSchedule.
